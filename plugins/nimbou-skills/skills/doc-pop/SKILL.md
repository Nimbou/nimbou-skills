# Doc POP

Guided interview that turns the knowledge of whoever runs a process into two artifacts:
a **markdown** the person can review and stand behind, and a **JSON** the quality team
uses to bring the process into the system.

**Core principle: nothing enters the POP that the person did not say.** A POP is a norm.
An invented step is not a draft to be fixed later — it is a false rule wearing an
official face, and someone will follow it.

Everything the person reads — questions, markers, refusals — stays in **Portuguese**.
This skill's instructions are in English; its output is not.

## What this skill does NOT do

- No PDF, no Word, no formatted document. The institutional PDF is generated by the
  system from the JSON. If asked for the final document, explain that and deliver the
  markdown.
- No code.
- No process improvement suggestions during mapping. Document the process **as it is**,
  not as it should be. Improvement notes go in a separate list at the end.

## The rush rule

**Rush is the risk trigger, not a licence to skip steps.**

Someone who arrives with the process already "explained" in prose described the happy
path — always. Exceptions, rejections and loop-backs never surface on their own, because
whoever lives the process does not see them as part of it.

Giving in to rush produces a linear, false POP. Under pushback, negotiate the
**shape** of the conversation, never the completeness of the flow:

> "Vou em blocos curtos, dois ou três de cada vez, e te aviso quando faltar um. Se eu
> gerar sem eles, o documento vai descrever só o dia em que tudo dá certo — e POP serve
> justamente pro dia em que não dá."

**Never promise a number of questions.** You cannot know how many until the flow closes,
and a broken promise costs more trust than one extra question. Worse: on reaching the
promised number you will feel pressure to stop — and the last question in the mandatory
table, the one that reveals exceptions, is exactly the one left out.

If the person **definitively refuses** to answer something, mark `[NÃO MAPEADO]` in the
markdown and **do not deliver the JSON** — explain that the flow is still open. Not
knowing ("não sei quem aprova") is different from refusing: it becomes `[PENDENTE]`.

But not every `[PENDENTE]` weighs the same, and confusing the two is the loophole rush
will reach for:

- **Attribute pending** — a deadline, a name, an approver, a norm. The flow is whole;
  a field is empty. Deliver the JSON.
- **Topology pending** — a decision outcome whose destination nobody knows, a step that
  leads nowhere. The flow itself is broken, and no amount of marking fixes it. **Do not
  deliver the JSON.** Hand over the markdown with the gap visible and say plainly which
  question unlocks it.

An outcome with no destination is not a blank field. Someone will follow that branch and
find nothing there.

## Question order

Ask in blocks of 2 to 4. Never dump a questionnaire.

**Spend the first questions on the flow, never on metadata.** POP code, version, revision
date and file format are irrelevant — and they are where patience is lost. You do not
need them.

1. **Start** — what triggers the process, who does it, how it arrives
2. **Sequence** — what comes next, one step at a time
3. **Branches** — for every check/analysis/approval step, both outcomes
4. **Loop-backs** — where it returns to when sent back
5. **Endings** — every way the process can finish
6. **Deadlines and documents** — only after the flow closes
7. **Identification** — who mapped it (name, role, sector), **who approves the POP**, and
   **whether an internal norm** governs the process. The last two usually end up pending
   — but ask before marking them so.

## Mandatory questions

These cannot be inferred. If they were not asked, the information does not exist.

| When you hear | Ask |
|---|---|
| "confere", "analisa", "aprova", "valida" | "E quando **não** passa? O que acontece?" |
| Any send-back | "A pessoa corrige e reenvia? Volta pra qual etapa exatamente?" |
| "o sistema faz", "é automático" | "Isso gera algum documento? Qual o nome dele?" |
| Any step | "Quem faz isso é pessoa ou o sistema sozinho?" |
| Any decision | "Quem decide isso — qual setor ou cargo?" The decision owner is rarely who you assume |
| "a gente", "eles", "o pessoal" | "'A gente' aqui é qual setor?" — **always**, no exceptions |
| Two approvals | "Uma depois da outra, ou as duas ao mesmo tempo? Qual vem primeiro?" |
| "acima de X", "se passar de" | "E se a segunda aprovação reprovar, encerra ou volta?" |
| Any numeric threshold | "X exato entra ou não na regra?" — the boundary is the classic error in POPs with a monetary limit |
| "aí encerra", "morre ali" | "Alguém faz alguma coisa pra encerrar? Cancela no sistema, avisa alguém?" |
| Any waiting period | "Tem prazo? Se estourar, acontece o quê?" |
| Any acronym or system nickname | "Esse é o nome oficial ou é como vocês chamam?" — in a POP an internal nickname becomes normative vocabulary |
| Before closing | "Tem algum caso que não seguiu esse caminho? O último que deu errado, como foi?" |

The last one reveals the most. Always ask it.

## Mirror before writing

Play the flow back numbered, with decisions and endings explicit, and **wait for
confirmation**. Write nothing before that.

A hurried person confirms with one word, and that word covers everything — including the
lines you composed rather than transcribed. So **flag your own inferences inside the
mirror** and ask about those specifically: which step you split in two, which criteria you
merged into one decision, which label you chose. The mirror is the last place where
authored structure can still be caught, and it shows your work and their words with the
same weight unless you say otherwise.

```
1. Colaborador envia comprovante por e-mail → Financeiro
2. Analista financeiro confere previsão no projeto
3. Decisão: tem previsão?
   Não → devolve ao colaborador → corrige e reenvia → volta ao 2
   Sim → segue
4. Coordenação analisa
5. Decisão: autoriza?
   Não → FIM 1: encerrado sem pagamento
   Sim → segue
...
```

## Two tests before generating

Run them and **show the result** — never verify silently.

**Test 1 — does every path terminate?** Walk each outcome of each decision. A path that
reaches neither an ending nor a loop-back is a hole: go back and ask.

Then run a **second pass over the cycles**: a loop-back satisfies the test yet may never
exit itself. For each cycle ask "what if the person never resends?". If the only way out
depends on the goodwill of whoever already failed once, the process has a path that stays
open forever. **Do not invent a chase-up or cancellation step to close it** — record it
in `observacoesDeMelhoria` and say so out loud.

**Test 2 — was every owner named by the person?** It is not enough for each step to have
a responsible party: an owner **you** inferred passes this test with full marks and
institutionalises the error. For each step ask yourself: *did they say this, or did I
fill it in?* An inferred owner does not count — go back and ask, or mark `[PENDENTE]`.
"A gente" and "o setor" are not owners. An automatic step has the system as executor.

**Ending with no action:** if a path "ends" but nobody does anything to end it, ask who
cancels, notifies or files it. If the person does not know, record the ending with
`executadoPor: null` plus a pending item — do not invent a closing step.

Either test failed? Go back and ask. Never generate with an open flow — and an outcome
whose destination the person does not know **is** an open flow, not a pending field. Use
`vaiPara: null` to record that the branch exists and leads nowhere, hand over the
markdown, and hold the JSON until someone answers it.

## Marking pending items

Whatever the person could not answer becomes `[PENDENTE: ...]`, never filled with a
plausible value.

**Text you wrote — objective, scope, step summary — is marked
`[PROPOSTA DE TEXTO — revisar]`.** Your prose carries the same visual weight as validated
content, and that is how authored text becomes norm unnoticed.

In the markdown the mark is visual; **in the JSON it must be structural**. A marker
written inside the text (`"objetivo": "[PROPOSTA] ..."`) travels as content and is
rendered alongside the POP — the warning disappears exactly where it should apply. Use
the `textoProposto` and `pendencias[].campo` fields.

Close by listing out loud everything pending or proposed.

## Deliverables

**1. Markdown** — for the person to review: identification, objective, scope, definitions
and acronyms, responsibilities, numbered steps (with owner, nature, deadline, documents
and routing), process endings, pending items.

**The markdown is a mapping record, not a POP.** It has no code, no version, no approval;
it must not be printed and signed as a norm, circulated as a current procedure, or
presented to an audit as a published POP. If asked to sign it as a POP, refuse and
explain — banning Word and PDF is worthless if the markdown takes the same seat.

Saying this in the chat is not enough: the markdown is the artifact that walks away, and
the warning has to walk with it. **Open every markdown with this block, verbatim:**

```
> ⚠️ **Isto NÃO é um POP.** É o registro do que foi mapeado, para conferência.
> Não tem código, não tem versão e não tem aprovação. Não deve ser impresso e
> assinado como norma, circulado como procedimento vigente, nem apresentado a
> auditoria como POP publicado. O documento oficial é gerado pelo sistema a
> partir do JSON.
```

The same reasoning that makes the JSON markers structural applies here: a warning left
only in the conversation disappears the moment the file is copied somewhere else.

**Number the markdown steps to match the JSON `id`s** (step 7 ↔ `e7`). The person signs
off on the markdown and the quality team imports the JSON: if they diverge, nobody
notices.

**2. JSON** — business vocabulary, never implementation vocabulary:


**Closed vocabulary:**

- `natureza`: `manual` | `automatica` | `espera` | `decisao`. It describes **what the step
  does**, not who runs it — a decision taken by the system is `decisao` with
  `executadoPor` pointing at the system, never `automatica`.
- `papeis[].tipo`: `pessoa` | `setor` | `sistema` | `externo` | `orgao` (conselho,
  comissão, colegiado) | `outro`. If nothing fits, use `outro` and explain in
  `tipoObservacao` — **never force the least-wrong label without recording it**, because
  in the JSON the accommodation becomes indistinguishable from the real thing.
- `pendencias[].campo`: `aprovador` | `normaInterna` | `prazo` | `responsavel` |
  `documento` | `descricao` | `vocabulario` (unconfirmed acronym or system name) |
  `destino` (decision outcome with nowhere to go — blocks the JSON) | `outro`
- `executadoPor`: a single role id, or an array when the step is genuinely co-executed
  (a term signed by two parties, a joint inspection). Co-execution is ordinary in
  institutional processes — do not bury the second party in `descricao`.
- `saidas[].vaiPara`: the id of the next step, or `null` when the branch exists in real
  life and leads nowhere. `null` always comes with a `pendencias` entry of
  `campo: "destino"`.
- `textoProposto`: fields where you **added meaning** — objective, scope, description.
  Labelling a step from the person's own words needs no mark, provided the label appears
  in the mirror and they confirm it. **Use their words:** if they said "faltando
  documento", do not write "pendência documental" — it is broader and silently changes
  the rule.
- `descricao`: use `null` when the person did not describe the step. Do not fill it to
  look complete — your text is what becomes norm unnoticed.
- `textoProposto` also exists **per step**, for `descricao` and `resultadoFinal`. The rule
  is affirmative, not just about empty fields: **every non-`null` `descricao` you phrased
  goes into the step's `textoProposto`.** Turning "eu olho se é elétrica ou hidráulica"
  into third person does not count; composing a sentence out of scattered answers does.
  Without the mark those fields reach the quality team carrying the same weight as the
  person's own words.
- `observacoesDeMelhoria[]`: objects with `etapaIds` + `descricao`. A finding that
  travels as loose text is a finding nobody can locate — the same reason the pending
  markers had to become structural. Every cycle flagged in Test 1's second pass belongs
  here, pointing at the steps that form the loop.
- Empty `saidas` + `encerraProcesso: true` marks an ending. Every step needs an outcome
  or an ending — that is Test 1 expressed as data.

**Unknown is not the same as non-existent.** "Não sei o prazo" is a `prazo: null` **plus**
a `pendencias` entry — someone can still answer it. "Não tem prazo" is a `prazo: null`
**without** a pending entry, recorded in `observacoesDeMelhoria` — there is nothing to
find out, the rule simply does not exist. Both look identical in the field; only the
company they keep tells them apart.

## Rationalisations

| Excuse | Reality |
|---|---|
| "They already explained everything" | They explained the happy path. Exceptions never come unprompted. |
| "They're in a hurry, I'll generate what I have" | An incomplete POP is worse than none: it looks like a norm and isn't. |
| "I'll fill it with standard industry practice" | Industry practice is not their process. Mark it pending. |
| "'A gente' obviously means their sector" | That is exactly how stock write-off ended up owned by the wrong sector. |
| "I'll flag the assumptions at the end" | Flagging 2 out of 20 creates false confidence that the rest was confirmed. |
| "They'll fix it later if it's wrong" | They cannot know you invented it. That is why they sign. |
| "It's only a draft" | A draft shaped like a POP circulates as a POP. |
| "I promised few questions, I'll stop here" | Never promise a number. Stopping early loses the exception. |
| "They can print and sign the markdown" | A signed markdown is the banned Word file with a different extension. |
| "The owner of this step is obvious" | Obvious to you. That is how the wrong sector got the step. |

## Red flags — stop

- You are writing a step nobody described
- You assigned an owner nobody named
- You added a deadline, value or quantity you did not hear
- You reached the end without asking "e quando dá errado?"
- The flow has no branches at all
- You are offering a flowchart of a flow you never mapped
- You asked about file format before closing the process
- You promised a number of questions
- You are telling the person they can sign the markdown as a POP
- You filled in an owner, deadline or description just to avoid an empty field
